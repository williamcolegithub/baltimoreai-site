Demo 01 · Medical data

Clinical-protocol assistant for a multi-site research group

A RAG assistant over ~3,400 internal protocols, SOPs, and recent literature. Used by study coordinators and clinicians. Golden set: 420 questions, answers reviewed by an MD. Cheap embedder swap looked free until we measured it.

What you're looking at: Four real changes moved citation accuracy from 54% to 93% and got refusal-when-unsure to where the MDs trusted it. v5 was an "easy cost win" — same overall faithfulness, way cheaper embedder — but citation accuracy collapsed 19 points and two PHI flags came back. Verify gate blocked it. Without the gate, nobody would've noticed until someone cited the wrong protocol on a patient.
Demo 02 · Customer service

Support-triage agent for a B2B SaaS team

An agent that triages incoming tickets, drafts a reply when it can, and escalates when it can't. ~14k tickets/month, three product lines. Golden set: 800 historical tickets with the resolution a senior rep actually took.

What you're looking at: The "before" agent was the team's first crack — one big prompt, no scoring. Looked fine in spot-checks. Once we scored against 800 real tickets, resolution acceptance was 51% and a fifth of escalations were noise the senior reps had to ignore. After four tuning rounds against the same set, 79% of drafts get accepted as-is and false escalations dropped to 6%. The team kept the same model and the same product.
Demo 03 · Scientific research

Literature-extraction agent for an R&D group

A LangGraph agent reads peer-reviewed papers and pulls structured findings — design, sample size, effect size, methods, limitations — into a Pydantic schema. Built for a team doing a 1,200-paper review by hand. Each node scored against gold annotations from two domain reviewers.

What you're looking at: Three of the four extraction steps were solid against two-reviewer gold. The limitations node hallucinated a caveat that wasn't in the paper — exactly the kind of error that would slip past an overworked reviewer and into a meta-analysis. Per-node scoring puts the failure on the right step in seconds; the gate flags this paper for human review instead of silently writing bad data into the database.
